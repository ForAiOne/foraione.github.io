THE PLACES

Saint-Erblon, Renazé, Longuefuye: three communes of South Mayenne.

THE MAP OF THE THREE VILLAGES

The setting fits on a road map: two villages to the west, one to the east of Château-Gontier.

“Implanté à saint erblon en Mayenne, non loin de Rennes et Angers.” It is the sentence of the archives, and it is enough to set the scene: everything happens in the south of the Mayenne département, in this part of Anjou attached to Mayenne in 1790, a few kilometres from Ille-et-Vilaine and Maine-et-Loire.

The rehearsal places, the villages where the musicians lived and the association that carried the concerts are spread over three communes: Saint-Erblon, Renazé and Longuefuye.

THE PLACES, IN ORDER

Six addresses, about fifteen years: the walls changed more often than the musicians.

1988 · Saint-Erblon

At Tanguy and Loran's grandmother's house

This is where it all begins. In this house in Saint-Erblon, the original quartet – the Rotoplo Fires, who will become Desastrax – records its first cassette: four tracks on a tape recorder of the time.

Early 1990s · Saint-Erblon

The basement of Régis and Tony's parents

The rehearsals go down to the cellar – “the basement, obviously”. It is the Explicit period, then No Mug: Gilles arrives on bass in 1991, Régis moves to drums, Loran to guitar, Tony to vocals.

1998 · Longuefuye, at les Courans

The egg-box studio

Régis takes a rental with a friend, Mickaël Lairy, in an outbuilding of the Courans manor. Enough room to set up a small rehearsal studio, sealed with egg boxes and pallets, and to prepare the Jar CD. (By the way, it is here that we were photographed by Loran to illustrate the cover of the Jar record.)

1999 · Renazé

Rue de l'Aubinière

A few months of rehearsals in a house on rue de l'Aubinière.

2001 · Craon

Near the espace Saint-Clément

A stop a little apart from the three communes of this page, about ten kilometres east of Renazé. (off the map) Craon is the central commune of the Pays de Craon, the intercommunality Saint-Erblon depends on: an old stronghold of 27 towers and 1,600 metres of walls, built to guard the Angevin border against Brittany, and a market town whose 12th-century halls were reputed among the biggest in France.

The name of the espace Saint-Clément is no accident: in the 9th century, the sister of Lambert, count of Nantes, ran a monastery in Craon dedicated to saint Clement.

From 2002 · Renazé

The TOUS AZIC'MUTS place

The rehearsals come back to Renazé, this time within the music association TOUS AZIC'MUTS, created with Hippy Groovy and the stylee mens – the two bands share the place. It is the time of the concerts in Renazé and of the Fête de la musique.

SAINT-ERBLON (53)

The village of origin

153inhabitants
100 %agricultural land
8 kmfrom Renazé
5 kmfrom Pouancé

Saint-Erblon is a village of South Mayenne, in what is called the Angevin Mayenne. The commune is classified “rural with very dispersed housing”: its land is today 100 % agricultural, between heterogeneous agricultural areas, arable land and a few meadows.

Its name comes from saint Herblon – saint Hermeland, monk-abbot of the diocese of Nantes who died in 720, invoked for the healing of paralytics. Under the Revolution, the commune was called Erblou-sur-Araize. Its inhabitants are the Saint-Erblonnais.

The village also has its legend. Saint Cénéré – the monk who came from Rome, evangelised Mayenne and settled in Saulges – was passing through Saint-Erblon in a time of great heat and drought. He asks the peasants for a little water; they, seeing their springs almost dry, refuse it to him. Carried away by anger, he strikes with his staff a stump that blocks the path: a limpid and fresh water springs from it. It is said that we still see the stump at the bottom of the fountain built above the spring – and it is this spring that is the origin of the pond.

The commune kept the fountain in its coat of arms, adopted on 2 December 1999: “vairé d'or et d'azur, à une fontaine de gueules”, where the fountain recalls precisely saint Cénéré.

In the Middle Ages the village was made only of a church and two houses, and the parish belonged to the barony of Pouancé. In the 17th century, rye was grown there on land cleared by the monks of a priory – a priory that would become the only café of the commune. The village then passed to the family of Ghaisne, counts of Bourmont.

In 1903, abbé Angot noted that the land registry plan of the commune, posted at the town hall, fitted on “a single sheet”.

The band

It is here that the band was based, and here that the very first rehearsals took place: in the house of Tanguy and Loran's grandmother, where Desastrax recorded its first cassette, then in the basement of the house of Régis and Tony's parents.

The 2004 archives locate Saint-Erblon “not far from Rennes and Angers”.

RENAZÉ (53)

The slate town and the association

2 504inhabitants
XVecentury: slate
3 564inhabitants in 1901
40 kmfrom Laval

Renazé is the biggest of the three communes: a rural small town about forty kilometres from Laval, on the Laval – Saint-Nazaire road axis, crossed by the departmental road 771. The commune touches Maine-et-Loire, and Brittany is not far: Eancé, in Ille-et-Vilaine, is about fifteen kilometres as the crow flies.

It is a slate town. From the 15th century, Renazé is a major place of the slate industry – the legend even makes saint Lézin, bishop of Angers in the 6th century, the first to have had the idea of covering roofs with slate stone, which earned him to be proclaimed patron of the slaters.

In 1850 the commune counted 300 workers. From 1880 to 1900 its population goes from 2,253 to 3,564 inhabitants, including 1,500 workers in 1900: a progression due to the improvement of the extraction methods and to the fact that the slate of Renazé was considered the best. The maximum of population is reached in 1901; the commune has 2,504 today.

Two passing dates: on 21 March 1793, Fouché, on a mission in Mayenne, indicates that Renazé is one of the communes “where people are imprisoned without any suspicion”. On 11 June 1856, Napoleon III comes to the town on foot, from the main road where the inhabitants, on their knees, had blocked his way.

The band

Renazé counts double in the story. We first rehearsed there for a while on rue de l'Aubinière, in 1999, before a stop in Craon – then the rehearsals came back here, within the association TOUS AZIC'MUTS: it is the town “that allowed the creation of the association”, of which Codéine was one of the two first members, alongside Hippy Groovy and the stylee mens, the two bands sharing the place.

We also find there the Fête de la musique, a concert in June 2002 and a date on 27 June 2003.

The village of the two castles

Longuefuye is a village of South Mayenne, eight kilometres from Château-Gontier. Since 1 January 2019 it is no longer a commune in its own right: it merged with Gennes-sur-Glaize to form the new commune of Gennes-Longuefuye, of which it is a delegated commune, with a delegated mayor. The merger was recorded by a prefectural order of 14 November 2018.

Under the Ancien Régime the parish depended on the Angevin sénéchaussée of Château-Gontier, itself attached to Angers. In 1790 the creation of the départements made this part of Haut-Anjou the south of Mayenne – the Angevin Mayenne.

The village has its heritage: two castles, that of la Villette (15th and 17th centuries) and that of les Courans (18th century). It also has its character, Henri-Augustin Girard de Charnacé (1777-1847), chouan and mayor of Longuefuye.

The band

It is the time of the Jar CD. Régis had taken a rental with a friend, Mickaël Lairy, in the middle of the countryside, in an outbuilding of the manor – at les Courans, the hamlet of Longuefuye, where the castle of the same name was precisely located.

There was room: enough to build a small rehearsal studio, sealed with egg boxes and pallets. It is there that the band prepared for the recording of the Jar CD – five tracks cut at Nono's, at the Panoramix studio, in Saint-Sauveur-de-Flée.

The record case carries besides the two addresses, like coordinates of youth, and its thanks mention Nono and Mickaël among the first.
